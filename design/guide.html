the protocol

Work the problem as if you won't solve it.

Then the goal is no longer the answer. It is a record of how you read the problem, which Competitor lays next to an expert's.

(i)

Mark the phrases

Copy the problem. Underline every phrase that seems to matter and write one line beside it: what does this suggest?

Circling "n+1" and noting "one more than the number of boxes".
(ii)

Say what it is about

One sentence, before any maths. What kind of problem is this, and what shape does it have?

"This is about forcing a pair, not finding one."
(iii)

Define the objects

Name what is given and what is wanted. Write the definitions you are relying on.

"Given S of size n+1; wanted a, b in S with gcd 1."
(iv)

List candidate methods

Two or three. For each, which phrase made you think of it and why you kept or dropped it. This is the stage the comparison cares about most.

"Pigeonhole, triggered by n+1 against n pairs; kept."
(v)

Attempt

Work the problem with your chosen method, a line of reasoning beside each step. Twenty to thirty minutes, then stop.

Dark pen, one page per photo, good light.
Then photograph your pages
Start an analysis

Why this works

Explaining each step in your own words forces you to notice what you actually used and what you merely skimmed. Reading the whole problem before solving stops the first idea from swallowing the constraints. Over time the phrases you once ignored become the cues you check first.
